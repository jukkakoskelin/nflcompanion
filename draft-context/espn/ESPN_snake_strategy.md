# ESPN 16-Team Snake Draft Strategy Template

## Strategy identity

- Strategy name: Balanced scarcity with RB/WR bench depth
- League: ESPN 16-team snake, 2026
- Primary objective: fill the starting lineup while building enough RB/WR
  depth to cover injuries, bye weeks, and the flex slot.
- Draft order: `[enter draft slot]`
- Scoring assumptions: `[link or reference verified ESPN scoring]`

## Roster construction target

The default end-of-draft roster is:

| Position | Target | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| QB | 2 | Starter plus one reliable backup |
| RB | 5 | Two starters, flex options, and injury/bye-week depth |
| WR | 4 | Two starters, flex options, and injury/bye-week depth |
| TE | 1 | Starter; avoid spending a bench slot unless value is exceptional |
| DEF | 1 | Starter; stream later if the draft board supports it |
| K | 1 | Starter; select late |
| **Total** | **14** | **9 starters plus 5 bench players** |

The RB/WR split may finish at 4/5 instead of 5/4 when the board makes one
position materially more valuable. The important constraint is to keep four or
five total bench RB/WR options after accounting for the starting flex.

## Minimum roster checkpoint

Aim to leave the draft with at least:

- 2 QB
- 2 RB
- 2 WR
- 1 TE
- 1 DEF
- 1 K

After those minimums are covered, use remaining selections primarily on RB and
WR. Do not force the exact target if the player pool or scoring settings create
a clear positional value advantage.

## Draft priorities

1. Secure difference-making RB/WR starters early because a 16-team league
   removes 15 players between the same picks and makes replacement value thin.
2. Track the flex as a real weekly starter, not as an extra roster slot. Prefer
   the stronger RB/WR value when deciding between filling the flex and adding
   depth.
3. Do not leave the draft without a usable QB2. The second quarterback is a
   required bench investment, not an optional luxury in this format.
4. Take TE1 when the tier or price is attractive; otherwise fill the scarce
   RB/WR pool and avoid carrying a second TE by default.
5. Draft DEF and K after the core skill-position roster unless the verified
   scoring settings make an exceptional value obvious.
6. Keep at least one late pick available for upside RB/WR depth rather than
   spending every bench slot on replaceable specialists.

## Position decision rules

### QB

- Draft one starter with a stable weekly role and one backup with either a
  strong floor or meaningful upside.
- Avoid taking QB3 or QB4 before the RB/WR target is complete.

### RB

- Prioritize workload, receiving involvement, and usable team context.
- By the end of the draft, target five total RBs unless WR value clearly
  justifies a 4-RB build.

### WR

- Prioritize target volume, route participation, and a stable role.
- By the end of the draft, target four total WRs unless the board supports a
  5-WR build.

### TE

- Fill the required TE slot with one player.
- A second TE requires a specific value, matchup, or trade rationale and must
  not displace a needed RB/WR bench player.

### DEF and K

- Draft one of each and generally defer both until the starting skill-position
  and QB requirements are satisfied.
- Do not draft a second DEF or K during the initial construction plan.

## Live-draft checkpoints

At each turn, record:

- Current round and overall pick
- Roster counts by position
- Remaining picks
- Best available RB, WR, QB, TE, DEF, and K tiers
- Number of teams still needing a starter at the position under review
- Whether the next pick should address a requirement or capture value

Use this checkpoint sequence:

1. Protect the minimum roster checkpoint.
2. Compare the best available player against the next likely tier drop.
3. Prefer RB/WR depth when QB, TE, DEF, and K requirements are already safe.
4. Recalculate the final 14-player target after every pick.

## Fades and guardrails

- Do not draft a second TE, DEF, or K while the minimum QB/RB/WR plan is
  incomplete.
- Do not use the injured reserve slot as a reason to draft an otherwise
  unusable player.
- Do not follow a fixed round-by-round script when the verified scoring or
  draft board changes positional value.
- Do not treat a projected starter as secure without checking role, injury,
  and team context in the imported player data.
